# History

## September 2020

[Wyess](https://github.com/Wyess) started
[shorthand_editor](https://github.com/Wyess/shorthand_editor), a browser-based
application for rendering Gregg and other shorthands based on typed text.

## July 2021

[chanicpanic](https://github.com/chanicpanic) created a [fork of
shorthand_editor](https://github.com/chanicpanic/shorthand_editor) dedicated to
Gregg Shorthand.

## March 2022

After the modeling of several joinings of "a" and "e" to consonants, the fork
had evolved significantly from its origin. Development on the project was
relocated to the Grascii organization at
[grascii/editor](https://github.com/grascii/editor).

## July 2022

Following months of active development, the editor had support for "o", "u",
"s", "th", "ng", "nk", and "i". However, even with a roadmap, development paused
indefinitely with v0.6.0-alpha0 as the last release.

## December 2024

[chanicpanic](https://github.com/chanicpanic) became aware of the potential
capabilities of modern font technology like OpenType...

## May 2025

[chanicpanic](https://github.com/chanicpanic) started
[grascii/font](https://github.com/grascii/font) using FontForge and initiated
the process of migrating the work from
[grascii/editor](https://github.com/grascii/editor) to the new system.

## September 2025

The migration of the work from the editor had completed, and development of new
glyphs and capabilities began.

## September 2026

[grascii/font](https://github.com/grascii/font) was made public and the first
version of the font was released as Version 0.070.
